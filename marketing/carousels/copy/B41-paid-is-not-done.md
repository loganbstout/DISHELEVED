## Post
The box arrived on time and I still had no idea whether the client was happy.

Before that, a different version of the same problem. A job that was finished, delivered and fully paid sat open on our board for weeks. Nobody forgot on purpose. There was simply no moment in anybody's day that said this one is over now.

So there are two false finishes. Paid, where accounting is satisfied and the board still shows work. And delivered, where the tracking says it landed and no human being has asked whether the thing was right.

Every number read off that board during those weeks was wrong, and it was wrong in the flattering direction.

The loop that closes it is five steps and only one of them is mine. Delivery lands and the job stops asking for attention. A week later the system drafts one short message. I read it, change a word, and press send. The answer files itself against the job rather than into my inbox. Then the job reads as closed.

Four steps ask nothing of anybody. Step three is the entire human contribution, and it exists because nothing here sends on its own. Six systems I have built write email and not one of them can press send.

That split is the only reason the loop will still be running in six months. Every field it needs is a field a machine already fills.

Pick your last twenty delivered jobs and count how many outcomes you actually know. Not the tracking status. The outcome.

The gap is never the sending. It is the remembering.
