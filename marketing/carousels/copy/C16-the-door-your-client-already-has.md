## Post
A client should never have to email a human being to find out where their own order is.

It is the most common question in this industry and the least interesting work in it. Somebody reads the email, opens three screens, copies a tracking number, and writes a reply that says in transit. The client did not want a reply. They wanted the answer.

So the client gets a door. A link and a company password, no seat to buy and no app to install. Behind it: their live jobs with a real status ladder, their past orders, carrier and tracking the moment it exists, and messages attached to the specific job rather than a shared inbox thread.

Proofs go out on a link with no login. That one detail is why proof decisions close out at 87 of 89, at a six minute median. The version that asked people to sign in and hunt for the file died at zero rows across six tables. Same team, same month.

What the client cannot see is deliberate. Not your cost, not your supplier, not your margin, not another client, not an internal note. The delivery paperwork that goes out has no money on it and physically cannot carry any.

Here is the honest part. The portal and the reorder shop are built and working, and they have not been handed to a client book yet. Two things are open first. Somebody has to be told when a client messages a job nobody owns, and the totals need to come from the invoice. Both are written on the page where my team can read them.

What does your client have to ask you for that they could simply be shown?
