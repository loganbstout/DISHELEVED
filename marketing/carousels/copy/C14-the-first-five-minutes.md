## Post
Nine months in, the fields my team had to type were still empty. The fields nobody touched were full.

I measured it instead of guessing. Every field that needed a person to type it sat between 0 and 15 percent filled. Every field a machine wrote sat between 50 and 100 percent.

The back planned deadline chain: 0 of 136. Confirmed in hands date: 0 of 136. Who has the ball right now: 2 of 136.

Meanwhile, proof decisions closed out at 87 of 89, and 71 of 136 jobs carried a full trail absorbed off a link somebody had already sent a client.

Same eleven people. Same nine months. The only difference was whether the software asked anybody for anything.

So a sales rep's day here is five things, and none of them are data entry. Forward the email you were going to forward. Post the update in the channel you already post in. Paste the link you already send the client. Say yes or no to a proof. Answer four short questions about a stale deal every fortnight.

That is the whole list. There is no sixth thing.

You can run this on your own software this afternoon. Open the tool you pay for, pick the five fields somebody promised would be useful, and count how many records actually have them filled. Then ask who was supposed to type them.

If the answer is your best employee, that field is empty and you already knew it.

Which field in your system is everybody quietly ignoring?
