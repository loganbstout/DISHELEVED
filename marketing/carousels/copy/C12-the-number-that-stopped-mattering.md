## Post
I carried a number around for five weeks like it was a debt. Then I went and looked, and it had already been paid.

Five weeks of low grade dread against the ninety seconds it took to check. Nobody was hiding it from me. I had decided in advance what the answer was going to be.

Here is how a stale number survives. You hear it once, in a hurry, from a system you trust. You build a plan around it instead of checking it. Then checking means admitting you planned on a guess, so the dread itself becomes the reason not to look. Meanwhile the number changes and nothing tells you.

Step three is where it gets expensive.

The number had been handled weeks before I got around to being afraid of it. The carrying cost was larger than the number, which is the part worth saying out loud.

So anything I am avoiding now gets a date and a source written next to it. If I cannot say where a figure came from and when, it is not a figure, it is a mood. And the check goes in the calendar the same afternoon, because the failure was never the arithmetic. It was the deferral.

Every shop has three of these. The receivable you are sure is still outstanding. The client you assume has left. The subscription you think you need.

One afternoon, three checks, and at least one of them is out of date.

This is the same instinct that made me count fill rates instead of trusting a research report, and that count killed a nine week plan. Going to look is the cheapest thing I do.

What is the number you have been carrying without checking?
