## Post
I wrote the brand standard myself. Then I shipped thirteen assets that broke it, and I did not catch a single one.

A colour per section, a shape per status, a fixed set of grounds, and a short list of things the copy never does. I wrote all of it down. Then I produced a run of assets at speed, reviewed them the way everybody reviews their own work, and pushed them out.

Thirteen breaks in one batch. Nothing exotic. A wrong ground colour. A banned word in a headline. A punctuation mark the house style does not use.

A human pass reads intent. You see what you meant to make, and you are the most familiar reader you will ever have. A machine pass reads characters. It has no idea what you meant, it does not get tired on asset nine, and it fails loudly.

So the rules became mechanical. No client name anywhere. No supplier tied to a named job. No margin, no pay, no money figures. No screenshot of a live book. Never open on the word everybody opens on. Zero of the one punctuation mark the house style avoids.

Six checks, read automatically over every asset and every line of copy, before anything gets scheduled rather than after it gets posted.

Your shop has the same problem in a different costume. The logo usage guide. The quote template. The rule about how a client name gets spelled. All of it lives in a document somebody read once.

Pick the rule that matters most and make it mechanical. A required field, a checklist that blocks sending, a template that will not let the wrong thing through.

If it needs somebody to notice, it is already broken somewhere.
